# How to Identify the Right Workflow Before You Buy AI

Most companies roll out AI backwards. They buy ChatGPT licenses first, then send a message to the team: "We have AI now. Find ways to use it."

A few enthusiastic people adopt it. Most try it twice and go back to the old way. Two months later, the owner is paying for seats nobody opens.

The tool wasn't the problem. The order was. **Identify the workflow first, then apply the tool to that workflow.** This post gives you the tools to do exactly that.

## The problem with starting from the tool

When you start with the tool, you ask: "What can we do with this?" That question puts the burden on every employee to invent use cases on top of their real job. The result is scattered, one-off experiments that are hard to measure and easy to abandon.

When you start with the workflow, you ask a different question: "Where is our work slow, inconsistent, or eating our best people's time?" Now AI has a specific job, an owner, and a way to prove it worked.

That shift is what turns AI from a novelty into more capacity, more time back for owners, and more consistent quality.

## Tool 1: The friction inventory

Before you look at any technology, collect the pain. Give your team (and yourself) ten minutes with these questions:

- What task do you dread every week?
- What takes much longer than it should?
- What gets redone because the first version wasn't right?
- What falls through the cracks when someone is out?
- Where do you wait on one person before work can move?
- What do customers complain about waiting for?

Write every answer down without judging it. You're building a list of candidate workflows, not a list of "AI use cases." Expect 10 to 20 items. Many will have nothing to do with AI, and that's fine.

## Tool 2: Frame the real problem

Complaints like "reporting is a nightmare" are too broad to solve. Turn each candidate into a specific, observable problem by walking through this chain:

1. **Trigger:** What starts the work? (A new lead, the end of the month, a customer request.)
2. **Specific pain:** What exactly goes wrong? (It takes six hours, it's inconsistent, it's late.)
3. **Who feels it:** Which role or team carries the burden?
4. **Current workaround:** How are people coping today? (Spreadsheets, copy-paste, staying late.)
5. **Business consequence:** What does it cost? (Lost bids, overtime, unhappy clients.)
6. **Better state:** What would "fixed" look like in plain words?

**Before:** "Proposals are a pain."

**After:** "When a new lead fills out our intake form, our account manager spends about three hours building a proposal from scratch. Quality varies by who writes it, and leads wait two to three days. We lose some deals to faster competitors."

The second version is something you can actually solve and measure.

## Tool 4: The eight-question workflow map

For your top two or three candidates, map how the work happens today in plain business language. No flowchart software needed. Answer eight questions:

1. **Who** performs the work?
2. **What** moves through the process? (A proposal, a request, a report, a customer question.)
3. **What starts** it?
4. **What information** does it need, and where does that information live?
5. **What steps** happen today, in order?
6. **What decisions** does someone make, create, approve, or hand off?
7. **What is the output** at the end?
8. **Why does it matter** to the business or the customer?

This is often the moment owners discover that much of the friction isn't technical at all. It's a missing template, an unclear handoff, or information scattered across five places. Fix those first, and AI has something solid to work with.

## Tool 5: Match steps to AI patterns

Now, and only now, bring in the tool. Look at each step in your map and ask whether it matches one of these common patterns where ChatGPT does well:

- **Summarize:** Turn long notes, transcripts, or documents into a short brief.
- **Draft and refine:** Create a first version of a recurring document, then improve tone and structure.
- **Extract and structure:** Pull key details from messy information into a table, checklist, or template.
- **Compare and recommend:** Lay out options, risks, and tradeoffs to support a decision.
- **Prepare a brief:** Build a meeting, account, or project brief from background material.
- **Review against a standard:** Check work against a checklist, policy, or style guide.
- **Coach through a process:** Guide someone step by step when expert help is limited.

In the proposal example, three steps match: summarize the intake notes, draft the scope from a template, and review the draft against a proposal checklist. The owner's approval stays human. That gives you a specific, testable instruction:

*"When a new lead completes the intake form, ChatGPT drafts the proposal from our template, current pricing, and the client's notes. The account manager reviews and edits it. The owner approves anything over $10,000."*

That sentence is the difference between "use AI more" and a workflow your team can actually adopt.

## Tool 6: Define value and readiness before you start

Pick one or two measures and record a baseline **before** you change anything. Useful categories include:

- **Time:** Hours spent per proposal, report, or request.
- **Speed:** Turnaround time from trigger to output.
- **Quality:** Revisions needed, errors caught in review, consistency between team members.
- **Capacity:** Volume handled by the same team, or clients served per month.
- **Experience:** Fewer late nights for the owner, faster answers for customers.

Then run a quick readiness check. Sort what you know into three columns:

Start with the highest total. Build it properly with templates, clear instructions, and a review step. Measure it for 30 days against your baseline. If the numbers move, expand to the next workflow. If they don't, adjust or move on.

## Red flags: When a workflow isn't ready for AI

- **Nobody owns it.** If no one is responsible for the result, no one will adopt the new process.
- **It happens once a year.** The setup effort won't pay back.
- **The process itself is broken.** AI will only make a bad process faster. Fix the handoffs first.
- **The data is off-limits.** If the inputs can't go into the tool under your policies, solve that before piloting.
- **There's no way to measure it.** If you can't tell whether it improved, you can't justify expanding it.

## Already bought the licenses?

You don't need to start over. Pick one workflow from your friction inventory, map it with the person who does it, and build it out with a template and a review step. Measure it for 30 days, then share the result with the team.

One working example does more for adoption than any number of training sessions.
